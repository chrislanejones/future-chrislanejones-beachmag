Site History: WordPress 2.1 to Next.js

2007-2009 Pre-chrislanejones.com

My first professional website was built on WordPress 2.1, released in January 2007. I modified the iconic Kubrick theme by Michael Heilemann—the default WordPress theme that defined an era of blogging.

The Kubrick theme was elegant in its simplicity: a clean white background, blue header, customizable header image, and that distinctive rounded sidebar. The domain was inspirationalexperiences.org.

2012-2016 - Chrislanejones.com: The Video Production Years

In 2013, I graduated with a Bachelor of Arts in Television Production after three video production communications internships at the University of North Florida.

I started my career in video editing working at a local church in Jacksonville, FL—First Baptist Church of Jacksonville. When I worked at the church, I spent hours designing my website and I decided to switch careers to building websites.

This video production website portfolio helped me land my first job in tech, even though it referenced me being a video editor and web designer.

I used a responsive theme to build my website, which was still a new concept in 2014. My first job in tech was at Web.com, and the majority of the websites were not responsive when I started.

This site was built with the CyberChimps Responsive WordPress theme—one of the early champions of mobile-first design in the WordPress ecosystem.

CyberChimps Responsive Theme Review

2017-2024 - WordPress Bootstrap Resume: The Ugly Years

I built my own WordPress website from scratch using Bootstrap, and honestly... it was ugly. I can't believe it was up for so long.

This website was live during my move to Virginia, a period of major life and career transition. Looking back, it's a humbling reminder that not every design decision ages well. The Bootstrap grid was there, the functionality worked, but the aesthetic? Let's just say it was more 'functional' than 'beautiful.'

Despite its appearance, this site served its purpose—it got me through contract work, networking events, and eventually led to better opportunities. It's proof that perfect is the enemy of done, and sometimes you just need to ship it and iterate later.

2024-2025 - The Short-Lived Post-COVID WordPress Site

This site was built during my transition from WordPress to React—a bridge between two worlds. I was falling in love with React's component model and growing increasingly frustrated with WordPress's direction, particularly the controversies surrounding Matt Mullenweg and the platform's fractured ecosystem.

The block editor (Gutenberg) felt clunky compared to the elegance of React components. The WordPress drama was exhausting. And most importantly, I wanted the creative freedom that React offered—especially for animations and micro-interactions.

React with Framer Motion made it trivially easy to add smooth, performant animations that would have been painful in WordPress.

This site didn't last long. By mid-2025, I had fully transitioned to a modern React stack with Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Framer Motion.

2025 - Modern React & Framer Motion

In 2025, I moved the site to Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Framer Motion.

I finally had the creative freedom I'd been craving. Framer Motion made animations smooth and intuitive. The component-based architecture meant I could build reusable pieces and iterate quickly. TypeScript caught bugs before they reached production. Tailwind made styling fast and consistent.

The bento grid layout, the smooth page transitions, the interactive components, the dark mode—all of this would have been significantly harder in WordPress. React gave me the tools to build the portfolio site I'd always envisioned.

Looking ahead, I'm excited to continue to push what's possible with modern web technologies. The journey continues.

2026 - The Beach Site

In 2026 we moved back to Florida after eight years in Virginia, and the site moved with us. I traded the mountain theme for the coast: sand and sea colors, an aerial shot of the Florida coastline on the home page, and a waves icon where the mountain used to be.

The stack stayed Next.js, React, and TypeScript, with Convex holding the content behind a custom admin. What changed was the feel. Every inner page opens on a drawn shoreline with drifting waves. Click it and a surfer rides through, then a dolphin jumps.

At the bottom of the home page there's a 3D sandcastle builder made with React Three Fiber. Flip a bucket, stack a tower on it, dig a moat, and top it off with a flag or a shell. Four corners and four walls make a fort.

It's the most fun I've had building this site, and it finally looks like where I live.
